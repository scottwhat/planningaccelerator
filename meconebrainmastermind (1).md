# The Mecone Brain — Strategy Mastermind
*Full specification: a stage-aware intelligence engine for the entire development lifecycle — August 2026*

---

## 1. The concept

The Brain is not a dashboard. It is a **stage-aware strategy engine**: it knows every project's exact position in the development lifecycle, holds a live model of every actor around it (minister, council, agencies, neighbours, media, market), remembers every lesson from every past engagement, and at each stage answers four questions:

1. **What do we need to know right now?** (the stage-specific intelligence)
2. **Who do we need, and who needs to be brought onside?** (the consultation & buy-in sequence)
3. **What are the plays?** (the moves available — technical, political, media, commercial)
4. **What did we learn last time?** (precedent from Mecone's own history and the market's)

The engine has three components: the **Radar** (finds opportunities before the market), the **Negotiation Layer** (models every variable around a live deal), and the **Playbook Library + Memory** (codified strategy, executed at the right stage). A project is a token moving through a state machine; each state transition fires the right intelligence, the right people, and the right plays.

## 2. The lifecycle state machine

### Stage 0 — Site identification (before the client exists)
**Brain tracks:** amalgamation patterns, SPV registrations, option caveats, corridor rezoning probability (council capacity shortfalls → forced upzones), TOD/UGA mapping changes, distressed assets, SEARs issued nearby.
**Insights out:** ranked acquisition-stage leads; "this corridor upzones within 24 months" calls; who owns what around every strategic site.
**Who to engage:** the *developer* (this is the outbound BD moment — partner call armed with the site's full trigger and precedent map); buyer's agents and land economists as referral partners.
**Play:** sell the pathway report *before* acquisition — get hired at option stage, price-setting, no competitor in the room.

### Stage 1 — Feasibility (the make-or-break 90 days)
**Brain tracks / supplies:** planning envelope (current + probable future controls), every SEPP Ch 4 referral trigger, bonus stack availability (infill affordable FSR/height, TOD provisions), council behaviour profile, comparable approvals (yield, height, conditions actually imposed), infrastructure servicing status (water/power/transport headroom), contribution liabilities (s7.11/7.12, HPC, likely VPA exposure), construction cost index for the typology, presale depth in the catchment, ghost pipeline (competing approved-unbuilt supply and its settlement timing).
**Insights out:** the **Feasibility Truth Pack** — realistic yield (not theoretical FSR), realistic program (council's *actual* median times, not statutory ones), realistic conditions cost (from the conditions library), pathway comparison (CDC / pattern-book / DA / panel / HDA-SSD) with probability-weighted timelines, and the kill-criteria ("walk away if X").
**Who to consult now:** quantity surveyor (cost reality), traffic engineer (scoping only — is there a fatal intersection?), a *soundings* conversation with council strategic planning (not DA staff — strategic staff reveal direction without creating a record against you), valuer for residual land value. **Not yet:** architects beyond massing, community, media.
**Lessons layer:** every past Mecone feasibility vs actual outcome — where did yield estimates miss, which councils' pre-lodgement signals proved unreliable.
**Play:** kill bad projects fast and cheaply — the credibility from telling a client *not* to buy is worth three engagements.

### Stage 2 — Strategy formation (choose the game before playing it)
**Brain supplies:** the full Site Dossier — political climate (minister priorities, local MP marginality, election calendar), precinct precedents, council posture, objection forecast, agency personnel sheet, infrastructure money map.
**Decisions the Brain frames:** pathway (and whether to *change* the game — rezone first vs DA under current controls vs HDA); staging (one big application or salami-sliced stages that each set precedent for the next); timing (lodge after the neighbouring precedent lands, outside pre-election freeze, before a DCP review tightens controls); positioning narrative (which of the government's own strategic goals this project delivers — the Sydney Plan language becomes the application's language).
**Who to bring in:** architect selected *for the audience* (panel projects reward design pedigree; HDA rewards delivery credibility; the Brain knows which architects have won with which panels/agencies before — a matchmaking dataset nobody else has); heritage/ecology early only if a trigger says so; a probity-clean government-relations advisor for HDA/SSD-scale projects.
**Buy-in sequence begins:** client's board/capital partner (lock the strategy so it survives pressure); then the quiet institutional allies — see the Coalition play, §4.3.

### Stage 3 — Pre-lodgement & council navigation
**Brain tracks:** who inside council/DPHI will touch the file (assessment officer histories, team vacancies, panel composition and each member's decision record), DCA 2-day pre-check results, pre-lodgement meeting outcomes vs what those same officers later did on other files (the "pre-lodgement reliability score" per council).
**Insights out:** the meeting brief — what this officer/panel has previously required, conceded, refused; which studies to bring finished vs which to hold; the questions to ask that lock council into positions on the record.
**Who to consult / when:**
- **Council DA staff** — formal pre-lodgement, with the conditions library already drafted into the scheme ("we've adopted your standard conditions") so the meeting is about the 3 real issues, not 40 defaults.
- **Councillors** — only via proper channels and only for rezonings/VPAs where they decide; the Brain tracks each councillor's voting record on density and their ward pressures.
- **DCA Front Door** — pre-lodgement for complex trigger sets; resolve inter-agency conflicts (RFS vs biodiversity) *before* they become conditions.
- **Neighbours & community** — for panel-decided projects, consult before lodgement so the objection count at exhibition is lower and the themes are pre-answered in the SEE; for HDA projects, calibrated lighter.
- **Local MP** — a courtesy briefing framed in jobs/homes numbers; not asking for anything, just removing surprise (surprised politicians oppose things).
**Play — getting the council to commit infrastructure (the road upgrade case, §4.1).**

### Stage 4 — Lodgement & assessment management
**Brain tracks:** the application's clock (statutory vs actual), every RFI across the council in real time (is our officer slower than their median?), referral status per agency vs league-table norms, objection inflow and themes as exhibition runs, panel agenda scheduling.
**Insights out:** escalation triggers ("agency X is 9 days past its own average — invoke the league table via the DCA Front Door"), RFI response strategy (answer narrowly; a broad answer restarts clocks and opens fronts), deemed-refusal countdown with LEC-appeal odds computed from that council's loss rate.
**Who now:** the full consultant orchestra responding to RFIs — but through one Mecone-controlled channel so answers are consistent (inconsistency between your own consultants is the most common self-inflicted wound; the Brain cross-checks draft responses against every prior statement on the file).
**Play:** exhibition-period media (§4.2) if objection volume threatens panel call-up; supporter-submission mobilisation (future residents, local business chambers, housing advocates — the YIMBY infrastructure now exists in Sydney and can be legitimately briefed).

### Stage 5 — Determination & negotiation
**Brain supplies:** the negotiation posture — strong levers here (precedent table, council's capacity shortfall, funded infrastructure nearby, the government's own announced targets), weak points (concede early what the conditions library says you'll lose anyway), each draft condition benchmarked against what the same authority imposed elsewhere ("this exceeds your own standard at X — justify or align").
**Who:** senior planner + lawyer only as needed (lawyering too early hardens positions); for panels, the Brain preps the 5-minute address around the three issues the specific panel members historically care about.
**Play:** the s34 conciliation route in LEC as a *negotiation venue* when council is stuck politically but staff agree technically — the appeal isn't hostile, it's a face-saving mechanism for council. The Brain knows which councils use it that way.

### Stage 6 — Post-consent activation
**Brain tracks:** every condition as a task-graph with owners and deadlines (deferred commencement items first), utility connection queues, works authorisation deeds, contribution payment timing options, mod probability (which conditions on this typology historically get modified — pre-plan the s4.55).
**Who:** certifier engaged early (the PC's interpretation of conditions varies — the Brain scores certifiers too), Sydney Water/Ausgrid case managers, DCA post-consent channel for stalled sign-offs.
**Play:** the "consent activation sprint" — a fixed-fee product that turns approved-but-stalled into construction-ready; escalation through the DCA's post-consent mandate as leverage.

### Stage 7 — Delivery & completion
**Brain tracks:** builder solvency signals on the client's contractor, occupation certificate blockers, design-change mod pipeline, defect/latent condition disputes that need planning input.
**Who:** ongoing light-touch retainer — planning input to construction-stage changes is small revenue but keeps Mecone on the file for the client's *next* site (Stage 0 for the following project starts here).
**Lessons capture (mandatory):** on completion, the Brain ingests the full delta — approved vs built yield, estimated vs actual program, forecast vs imposed conditions cost, which plays worked. This closes the loop that makes every future Stage 1 Truth Pack sharper.

## 3. The buy-in matrix — who, when, what for

| Actor | When | What you want | What they want (the trade) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Client board / capital | Stage 2 | Strategy lock, patience budget | Certainty narrative, staged capital calls |
| Council strategic planners | Stage 1 | Direction signals, no record | Projects that deliver their housing targets |
| Council DA staff | Stage 3 | Clean assessment, few RFIs | A complete application in their own format |
| Councillors | Stage 3 (rezoning/VPA only) | Votes | Ward wins: public domain, the gap-list item |
| DCA / agencies | Stage 3–4 | Conflict resolution pre-conditions | Referral-complete packages, no clock games |
| Local MP | Stage 3 | Neutrality (not support) | No surprises; jobs/homes framing; site visit |
| Minister / DPHI executive | Stage 2 (HDA-scale only) | Declaration, announcement appetite | Their strategy delivered: starts, affordable %, jobs west |
| Community | Stage 3–4 | Lower objection volume | Genuine design responses on the top 2 themes; local benefit visible early |
| Media | Stage 4 (only if needed) | Frame control | A story: homes for workers, first-home buyers, jobs |
| Future occupants / demand side | Stage 4 | Supporter submissions | A voice — they are the unrepresented majority in every DA fight |

The rule the Brain enforces: **buy-in is sequenced, not simultaneous.** Capital before council; strategic staff before DA staff; MP before minister; community before exhibition; media last and only when the objection forecast demands it.

## 4. The playbook library (codified plays, fired by stage + conditions)

### 4.1 Getting a council to commit road upgrades for a housing project
The wrong way: ask council to fund your intersection. The Brain's sequence:
1. **Evidence:** TfNSW volume data shows the intersection fails *today*, without the project — existing deficiency, not development impact.
2. **Money map:** the council's s7.11 plan already lists the upgrade with $X collected and unspent (public registers) — or the corridor sits in an HPC zone, making it a state-side obligation.
3. **Obligation frame:** council's Sydney Plan capacity benchmark requires *feasible, serviced* capacity — an unserviced corridor is their compliance gap, quantified by the Readiness Index.
4. **The offer:** works-in-kind — the developer builds the upgrade now, offset against contributions; council gets a delivered asset years early at no political cost and announces it as their win.
5. **Escalation if refused:** the DCA/Gateway record now shows council declining a funded, listed, deficiency-fixing upgrade while failing its capacity benchmark — a position that does not survive state review. Never say this aloud; let the record say it.

### 4.2 Media & narrative campaigns (defensive and offensive)
- **Default: silence.** Media entry is a decision the Brain gates on the objection forecast and decision-maker type (panels read papers; delegated officers don't).
- **Defensive:** when a resident campaign forms, don't argue with it — reframe the population. Objectors are ~50 households; the project's constituency is 300 future households who don't know they exist yet. Human stories (the nurse who could live near the hospital), supplied to journalists who cover housing scarcity, out-frame "overdevelopment" coverage.
- **Offensive (rezoning/precinct scale):** commission and publish the economic study ("X homes, Y jobs, $Z"), place it via the industry bodies (UDIA, Property Council, Committee for Sydney — Mecone's existing relationships) so the argument arrives with institutional weight, not developer branding.
- **Rule:** every public number must match every application number. The Brain cross-checks; inconsistency is how campaigns die.

### 4.3 Pressuring government to support the projects behind its own strategy
The state has published its targets (800k homes, jobs west, 30-yr feasible capacity). That creates a lever private actors rarely use systematically:
- **Mirror their language:** every submission, EOI and media line expressed as delivery of *their* plan — a minister can't dismiss their own KPIs.
- **Aggregate the evidence:** Mecone's portfolio = a private pipeline dataset. "These 14 projects = 9,200 homes, stalled on these 6 specific blockers" is a submission no individual developer can make and DPHI cannot ignore — and it positions Mecone as the industry's data voice (the Committee for Sydney channel again).
- **League-table activism:** publicly celebrate fast agencies, factually note slow ones. The DCA's own transparency instrument becomes the pressure mechanism — no lobbying required, just arithmetic.
- **Offer the announceable:** governments need ribbon-cuttings and start-of-works events on a political calendar. A project that can start within an announcement window has trade value; the Brain tracks the political calendar as an asset class.

### 4.4 Precedent farming
Deliberately sequence small approvals to build the precedent ladder for the big one: the 6-storey approval that normalises the 12; the boarding-house consent that establishes the traffic position. The Brain identifies which precedent each target site needs and whether a cheaper vehicle can set it first.

### 4.5 The coalition play
For precinct-scale moves, assemble aligned interests before exhibiting anything: neighbouring landowners (shared infrastructure cost, unified voice), the local business chamber (jobs framing), housing advocates, the university or hospital anchor whose workers need the homes. The Brain's entity graph shows who owns what nearby and therefore who gains from your uplift — your natural, self-funding allies.

## 5. The lessons engine (institutional memory as infrastructure)

Every engagement writes back structured records: what was forecast vs what happened (yield, program, conditions, costs); which officer/panel/agency did what; which plays were run and their outcomes; what the client accepted or refused and why. Retrieval is stage-triggered — a planner opening a Stage 3 pre-lodgement brief for Ku-ring-gai automatically sees every prior Mecone interaction with that council, that officer, that panel, and the three lessons tagged to them. Partners' 25-year memories stop walking out the door at retirement; every new hire negotiates with the firm's entire history behind them. This — more than any model — is the moat: **competitors can copy the scrapers; they cannot copy fifteen years of structured outcomes.**

## 6. Worked example — "10–14 Thomas Street, Chatswood" through the lifecycle

*Fictional but calibrated to real Chatswood conditions: a ~35-storey, ~380-apartment shop-top housing tower, CIV ~$185m, three amalgamated lots 400m from Chatswood metro, B4 mixed-use, adjoining a heritage-listed shopfront, site partly within a classified-road frontage. Client: a mid-tier developer ("Helix Property") new to Mecone.*

### Stage 0 — Site identification (month −6 to 0)
The Brain flags the play before Helix is a client: two of the three Thomas St lots transfer to related SPVs ("10 Thomas St Pty Ltd", "12 Thomas St Pty Ltd" — same registered office as Helix's director) within four months; an option caveat appears on the third. Entity resolution links the SPVs to Helix. The Radar scores it: amalgamation complete-pending (0.9), corridor precedent strength high (six 30+ storey approvals within 600m — Mecone acted on four), no incumbent planner detected on any Helix entity, estimated fee $280–380k. **Action fired:** partner outreach with a teaser pathway snapshot — "your site can likely reach ~35 storeys under the HDA pathway; three referral triggers; we ran the corridor." Helix engages at option stage — before the third lot settles.

### Stage 1 — Feasibility (months 0–2)
**Truth Pack generated in 4 days (was 3 weeks):**
- *Envelope:* LEP allows ~90m; corridor precedent supports ~120m via HDA-SSD with design uplift; infill affordable bonus (12% affordable → +25% FSR) takes yield from ~310 to ~385 apartments. CIV $185m → clears the $60m HDA threshold easily.
- *Triggers (SEPP Ch 4):* TfNSW consultation (classified road frontage — *designed out* by relocating vehicle access to the side street: one referral deleted, 28+ days and condition risk removed); Heritage NSW consultation (adjoining item — manageable, podium setback strategy from the 44-52 Anderson St precedent); Sydney Water (standard).
- *Council reality:* Willoughby's median for 20+ storey DAs = 14 months, panel refusal rate 38%, two current councillors elected on anti-tower platforms → **HDA pathway strongly indicated**; probability-weighted program 8 months vs 16.
- *Conditions cost forecast:* $4.1m (from the conditions library: public domain works, through-site link, art contribution) — priced into the residual land value *before* Helix exercises the option.
- *Kill-criteria check:* ghost pipeline shows 1,850 approved-unbuilt apartments in Chatswood but settlement evidence is strong post-rate-cuts; presale depth adequate. **Verdict: proceed, HDA pathway, exercise the option.**

### Stage 2 — Strategy formation (months 2–3)
Site Dossier compiled. Key strategic calls the Brain frames:
- *Pathway:* HDA-SSD. Affordable component set at 12% (clears both the bonus threshold and the HDA's contribution test — yield-accretive, and gives the minister an announceable number).
- *Timing:* lodge EOI in month 4 — after the neighbouring Anderson St determination (expected month 3) sets the 120m precedent, and 14 months clear of the state election window.
- *Narrative:* "385 homes including 46 affordable, 400m from metro" — pure Sydney Plan language (housing east, TOD delivery). Every document will carry the same three numbers.
- *Team matchmaking:* the architect dataset shows two practices with 100% success before DPHI design review on Chatswood towers; Helix's preferred architect has never faced state design review — Brain recommends pairing them with one of the two as design-review lead. Heritage consultant chosen from the two whose Chatswood podium strategies Heritage NSW has previously accepted.
- *Buy-in sequence locked:* Helix board (this month, strategy memo) → council strategic planning soundings (month 3 — informal, no record) → DCA pre-lodgement (month 4) → local MP courtesy brief (month 5) → community preview (month 5–6, before exhibition).

### Stage 3 — Pre-lodgement & navigation (months 3–6)
- HDA **EOI lodged month 4; declared month 5** (the Brain's EOI draft mirrors the HDA's published assessment criteria clause-for-clause: tenure secured, 9-month lodgement commitment, builder term sheet showing 12-month start capability, 12% affordable).
- **DCA pre-lodgement:** the heritage-vs-density tension raised and resolved *now* — Heritage NSW accepts the 3-storey podium interpretation in writing before SEARs; no late condition surprise. TfNSW confirms in the 2-day pre-check that the redesigned access needs no referral.
- **Willoughby Council:** not the consent authority, but the Brain's councillor profile predicts a hostile submission at exhibition. Counter-play: the money map shows council's s7.11 plan lists the Thomas St pedestrian link with $2.8m collected, unspent. Mecone offers it as **works-in-kind** — Helix builds the link, offset against contributions. Council staff now have a delivered asset to announce; the eventual council submission criticises height but *supports the public domain package* — a split position that reads weak at assessment.
- **Community:** objection forecast = 60–90 objections, themes ranked traffic (1), overshadowing (2), heritage (3). Pre-lodgement community preview addresses traffic head-on with the intersection data (it fails today; the project funds signal upgrades), and the design pack shows winter solstice shadows clearing the key open space. Actual objections at exhibition: 41.
- SEARs issued month 6 — no surprises, because every likely requirement was pre-answered in the DCA pre-lodgement record.

### Stage 4 — Lodgement & assessment (months 6–12)
- SSDA lodged month 8, **referral-complete** (flood, traffic, heritage, wind, all to State Referral Provisions standard) — the DCA's 28-day clock runs once and never pauses.
- The Brain's live tracker flags one drift: the DPHI assessment officer's RFI is 11 days past that team's median → escalated through the DCA Front Door with the league-table framing; response lands 3 days later.
- Exhibition: 41 objections, one resident-group Facebook campaign. Media gate stays **closed** (delegated DPHI assessment, no panel — media adds risk, no benefit). Supporter play instead: 63 supporting submissions mobilised via the local business chamber and a housing-advocacy group briefed with the three numbers.
- Willoughby's split submission lands as predicted; the response-to-submissions leans on the works-in-kind package and the corridor precedent table.

### Stage 5 — Determination & negotiation (months 12–14)
- Draft conditions arrive. The Brain benchmarks all 94 against the consent authority's own record: 88 standard, 4 pre-agreed, **2 outliers** — a public-art contribution 3× the Chatswood norm and a road-dedication condition inconsistent with the TfNSW pre-check. Both challenged with citations to the authority's own precedents; both aligned within a fortnight.
- **Consent granted month 14** — versus the 24–30 months the same building would have taken through council pre-reform. Approved: 385 apartments, 46 affordable, 119m.
- The minister's office takes the announcement (offered in Stage 2's narrative design); Helix gets a launch story; Mecone gets the case study.

### Stage 6 — Post-consent activation (months 14–18)
- Conditions decomposed into a 94-node task graph. Deferred-commencement items (contamination validation, works-in-kind deed) sequenced first; Sydney Water case manager engaged in week 1 (the Brain's queue data shows a 4-month connection backlog — starting now, not at CC stage, saves the program).
- One stall: the works authorisation deed for the signal upgrade sits in a TfNSW queue 6 weeks — escalated via the DCA post-consent channel, cleared in 2.
- CC issued month 18; construction starts month 19 — inside the HDA's 12-month commitment, protecting Helix's standing for its *next* declaration.

### Stage 7 — Delivery & lessons capture (months 19–48)
- Light retainer: two s4.55 modifications (facade material, basement layout) predicted by the mod-probability model and pre-scoped.
- **Lessons written back:** actual vs forecast — program 14 months vs 13 predicted; conditions cost $3.9m vs $4.1m forecast; the works-in-kind play converted a hostile council into a split submission (tagged, reusable); the architect pairing prevented a design-review cycle (est. 3 months saved). All tagged to Willoughby, DPHI team, and the Chatswood corridor for the next project's Stage 1.
- **Stage 0 restarts:** Helix, now a proven client, is shown the Radar's next three Chatswood-corridor opportunities before they hit the market. Lifetime value: one engagement → a pipeline relationship.

**The counterfactual line for the pitch deck:** same building, old system, council pathway — 24–30 months, design competition, VPA ambush, ~$3–5m extra holding cost, and a planner hired *after* the land was bought at the wrong price. The Mastermind's value is the delta.

## 7. Revenue mechanics of the Mastermind

- **Premium capture:** Site Dossiers and Truth Packs justify top-of-market fees on major engagements — clients pay for certainty, and certainty is now manufactured.
- **Earlier, wider scope:** engaged at option stage (Stage 0–1) instead of post-acquisition — DD, strategy, DA, post-consent, next site. Lifetime value per client multiplies.
- **Higher win/approval rates → reputation → inbound.** The flywheel's demand side.
- **Productised slices:** feasibility Truth Packs and acquisition-stage dossiers sold to bidders before any DA engagement exists; council-behaviour subscriptions; the aggregated-pipeline submissions that make Mecone the industry's evidence broker.
- **Margin:** fewer partner hours per outcome; juniors executing with senior-grade context; bad projects killed before they consume the practice.
- **Defensibility:** the lessons engine compounds and cannot be bought.

**Build sequence:** the state machine + lessons capture first (it's a discipline, then a database, then a product) → council behaviour profiles + precedent tables (computable now from portal data) → the Dossier as a generated document → stage-triggered playbooks → scoring models last. Pilot on the Chatswood book where ground truth is richest.

**Honest limits:** the Brain recommends; humans decide — political and media plays especially need judgment the model should inform, never execute. Client-confidential data needs hard walls between engagements. And lobbying/political engagement must stay inside NSW lobbying and donations law — the Brain should track compliance obligations as one more dataset.
